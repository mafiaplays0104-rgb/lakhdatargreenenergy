General Biomass Energy & Co-Firing FAQs

Get immediate, expert answers regarding high-GCV biomass pellets, thermal efficiency, boiler retrofitting, and environmental compliance for India's heavy manufacturing and thermal power sectors.

Biomass energy is thermal energy derived from organic materials like agricultural residue, wood waste, and crop husks. At an industrial scale, these raw materials are dried, milled, and densified into high-GCV (Gross Calorific Value) pellets. These pellets serve as a renewable, carbon-neutral alternative to fossil fuels, engineered specifically to be co-fired or used as a complete drop-in replacement in existing coal-fired boilers and thermal power plants (TPPs).

Utilizing biomass pellets directly prevents toxic open-field stubble burning by giving farmers a commercial outlet for surplus crops. At the industrial level, it replaces heavy coal consumption and reduces harmful SOx and NOx emissions by an impressive 60-80%. Furthermore, because biomass absorbs CO₂ during its growth cycle, it is officially classified as a carbon-neutral fuel. This allows industrial facilities to slash their Scope 1 emissions, improve ESG ratings, and strictly comply with Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) guidelines.
