Understanding High-GCV Biomass Pellets: A Complete Technical Guide

Lakhdatar Editorial 12 min read Jul 15, 2026

What Is Gross Calorific Value (GCV)?

Gross Calorific Value measures the total heat energy released when a fuel is completely burned. For biomass pellets, GCV is the single most important performance metric — it determines how much thermal energy your boiler extracts per kilogram of fuel consumed.

Lakhdatar Green Energy pellets consistently deliver 3,800–4,200+ kcal/kg GCV, verified through batch-by-batch laboratory testing.

Raw Material Selection & Impact

The calorific value of a biomass pellet depends directly on its source material. Sawdust pellets, mustard husk pellets, and mixed agro-waste pellets each have distinct combustion profiles.

Sawdust pellets typically deliver the highest GCV consistency due to the uniform cellulose structure of wood fiber.
  • Sawdust Pellets: 4,000–4,200 kcal/kg — low ash, clean combustion
  • Mustard Husk Pellets: 3,800–4,000 kcal/kg — abundant supply from Rajasthan and MP
  • Mixed Agro-Waste: 3,600–3,900 kcal/kg — cost-effective, high availability

Quality Parameters That Matter

Beyond GCV, three parameters determine whether a pellet will perform reliably in your industrial boiler:

  1. Ash Content (< 10%): Lower ash means less clinker buildup and reduced maintenance.
  2. Moisture (< 10%): Excess moisture directly reduces effective calorific value.
  3. Ash Fusion Temperature: Higher AFT prevents slagging inside the combustion chamber.

Biomass vs Coal: Performance Comparison

While coal delivers higher raw GCV (5,000–6,000 kcal/kg), biomass pellets offer compelling advantages that offset the energy density gap. Biomass is carbon-neutral, produces 60–80% less SOx/NOx emissions, and is increasingly cost-competitive as carbon pricing mechanisms expand across Indian states.
